Applications · Recorded results

Recorded applications

RF-TraceGuard has been applied at three sites with different equipment, frequencies and measured quantities. Each time the software flagged a persistent change, engineers at the site found the physical cause, and a repeated measurement confirmed that the path had recovered.

The records below come from the acts and reports issued by the host organizations. Organization names, staff names and site-specific equipment details are kept confidential. The figures are those recorded at the time.

Reading these cases

Each case describes a documented event: the software record, the search for the cause and the confirmation of recovery. Each figure belongs to its own event; a general detection rate or system accuracy calls for a larger study.

Overview

Overview of the recorded cases

Each case started from the same engineering question: whether something in the measuring chain had changed since its last check, and where. The sites use very different equipment: a television assembly line at a few hundred megahertz, a microwave laboratory working up to 18 GHz, and a university test bench that records electric current.

Case 01 / Electronics manufacturing

A shared cable path drifted while products kept passing

The site is a contract manufacturer of consumer television equipment. An automated station checks the radio receiving section of the sets on a sampling basis; between 80 and 150 units pass the station per shift. The test signal runs from an RF generator through a programmable attenuator and an RF switch into a common 75-ohm coaxial path, and from there into the antenna input, tuner and DVB-T2 demodulator of the set under test.

RF-TraceGuard was evaluated on this station from 1 to 5 September 2025. It ran on site in read-only mode. The approved test procedure stayed as it was, the station kept making its own pass or fail decisions, and the software received copies of the results and of the reference measurements, taken at least twice per shift and after any corrective action.

Fig. 1. Reference loss of the shared path at 666 MHz during the evaluation week. The dashed vertical line marks the corrective action. Values are taken from the record attached to the evaluation act.

The loss at 666 MHz rose steadily for four days and crossed the warning level on the afternoon of 4 September, before it reached the critical level and before any product failed. At 198.5 MHz the same path changed by only +0.04 dB. A deviation that grows with frequency and appears on every product routed through the same cable points to the path itself.

The production verdict stayed PASS for all affected units. RF-TraceGuard tied the deviation to a defined window of 12 products and 24 measurements and named the common cable and its RF connections as the most probable source. Engineers reseated the connectors, replaced the cable and repeated the reference measurement. It read 2.14 dB at 666 MHz, a residual deviation of +0.04 dB, and the warning cleared.

Before the evaluation, this kind of station drift was usually noticed through a rise in failures or at the next calibration, and the search for its cause meant checking the generator, the switching and the cable path one after another. The site's management put that search at 1.5 to 3 hours. In the six months that followed, the software was used on four inspection stations, and the manufacturer reported 15 further cases of a changed measurement path that it detected and localized, each with its own record kept in the system.

Case 02 / Microwave measurement laboratory

A network analyzer setup drifted at the top of the band

The laboratory runs automated microwave measurements on several comparable two-port vector network analyzer setups. The pilot ran from 18 August to 14 September 2026 on three of them, working from 1 to 18 GHz with 3.5 mm coaxial connections. Before the first series, each setup received the same full two-port calibration with short, open, matched load and thru standards.

A fixed 10 dB coaxial attenuator served as the control device and was always connected in the same direction. In every series each setup measured it ten times on a 201-point frequency grid, and the results were saved as Touchstone files. The reference profile was formed once, from series 1 to 3, as the mean S21 trace of the three setups at each frequency point, and it stayed fixed for the rest of the pilot.

Series 11 and 12 follow the cable replacement and a full two-port recalibration. The dashed horizontal lines are the experimental control levels of the pilot; the metrological tolerances of the setups remain in force.

Stations A and B stayed inside their initial scatter for the whole period. Station C began to move away from the reference profile in series 5, mostly between 12 and 18 GHz. It crossed the warning level in series 7 and the critical level in series 9. In series 10 its deviation reached 0.38 dB in magnitude and 4.1° in phase, and 47 of the 201 frequency points were above the warning level.

Station staff then checked station C step by step. They reconnected the control attenuator, verified the calibration, inspected and cleaned the connectors, recalibrated, and replaced the elements of the path one at a time. Replacing the cable on port 2 brought the deviation down from 0.38 dB to 0.07 dB. The removed cable was afterward measured on its own against a spare cable of the same type, after a fresh calibration.

The separate check showed the loss and phase error of the old cable growing with frequency, up to 0.31 dB and 3.4° at 18 GHz. Because every series had been kept, the start of the change could be placed at series 5, and the retrospective review covered six measurement cycles of station C, a small part of everything measured since the last scheduled calibration. Over the twelve series the software handled 72,360 values for each monitored parameter.

Case 03 / University research laboratory

A loose shunt connector on an electromechanical test bench

This application lies outside RF and shows the same method on a different physical quantity. A university laboratory runs repeated cycles on an experimental electromechanical bench and records the drive current through a measuring shunt. The raw data, sampled at up to 4 kHz, were exported from the bench data logger as CSV files and processed with RF-TraceGuard version 0.9.2. The monitored quantity was the mean absolute current over a 60-second steady-state section of each cycle.

The processing was the same as on an RF station: a baseline formed from qualified cycles, an EWMA with a control limit, and a check of the cause on the hardware once the limit was crossed.

Baseline series12 cycles at each of two modes, 1.5 Hz and 1.8 Hz, 26 to 30 January 2026
Baseline at 1.8 HzMean 1099.6 mA, standard deviation 9.6 mA (0.87%)
Trend filterEWMA, λ = 0.20, control limit at L = 3; the upper limit settles at 1109.2 mA
Bench temperature before recording42.0 to 42.7 °C across all series

Fig. 4. Series of 2 February 2026 at 1.8 Hz. The EWMA crosses its upper control limit at the fourth cycle and keeps rising until the end of the series. Values are taken from the laboratory act.

On 2 February 2026 eight consecutive cycles were recorded at 1.8 Hz. The EWMA crossed its upper control limit at the fourth cycle and stayed above it, still rising, to the end of the series. The last cycle read 1141.5 mA, 3.81% above the baseline mean. The bench temperature stayed between 42.1 and 42.7 °C and held steady, which rules out heating as the cause. The software classed the series as a sustained positive offset.

The next day staff checked the measurement circuit and found that the signal connector of the shunt circuit had worked loose. They disconnected it, cleaned the contacts, reconnected it and fixed it mechanically. With the drive stopped, the zero reading of the channel fell from 12.4 to 1.6 mA. The fault had shifted the zero of the channel and also changed the effective gain of the shunt circuit, which is why the deviations grew during the series. A repeat series of six cycles on 4 February stayed within +0.78% of the baseline, inside the normal scatter of the bench.

Scope of the evidence

Conclusions from the recorded cases

In every recorded case the software saw a persistent change before it turned into a hard failure, narrowed down where to look, and kept the history needed to decide which earlier results had to be reviewed. In each case the repair decision stayed with the site: its own staff found the physical cause and confirmed it with a repeated measurement.

These records are documented examples, and a statistical validation needs a longer series of applications. The thresholds were set separately for each site, and the diagnostic ranking serves as an aid to inspection. The method itself is described on the Technology page, and its relation to existing tools on the Comparison page.
