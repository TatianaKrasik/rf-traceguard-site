Comparison · Existing tools and methods

RF-TraceGuard and the existing tools

Engineers have watched over test stations for a long time. Calibration management, production analytics, decision-risk metrology and built-in self-test each cover part of the problem. This page sets out the scope of each and the point where RF-TraceGuard differs.

The comparison rests on a structured search of patents, metrological standards and published product documentation, completed on 31 August 2026 and extended on 21 September 2026. Products change, so each entry reflects its public documentation at that time.

Established methods

RF-TraceGuard relies on well-known tools: EWMA, CUSUM, root-sum-square uncertainty, coverage factors, guard bands and golden-unit checks. All of them are established methods. The difference lies in their object: the physical measurement path behind an individual production result.

01 / The engineer's questions

Questions an engineer asks about a station between calibrations

An engineer needs to know whether a particular measurement can carry the pass or fail decision on a particular unit, given the uncertainty of its path, the current condition of that path and the distance from the measured value to the nearest specification limit. When the station drifts, the engineer also needs to know which component changed: a cable, a connector, a switch branch, a fixture or the analyzer.

Established tools answer neighbouring questions: whether the instrument is due for calibration, whether the yield of a line is moving, whether the uncertainty of a procedure is acceptable. Those answers are useful, and the engineer starting a shift still needs to know whether this morning's results can be trusted and where to look first.

The closest RF-specific item is the Rohde & Schwarz patent. A verification against known standards gives the effective directivity, source match and reflection tracking of an analyzer, and the uncertainty of the current measurement appears with the result. Its scope ends at the analyzer's calibration plane: cables, switches and fixtures beyond that plane, their history and the product tolerance lie outside it.

Averna Proligent Analytics and Fluke MET/CAL often come up in the same discussion. Averna Proligent Analytics consolidates results across stations and offers yield, Cpk, trend and drill-down analysis. Fluke MET/CAL applies measurement uncertainty and guard banding to calibration decisions, including the system uncertainty of instruments, cables and connectors. Both are strong in their own field, and both work at the level of the line or the instrument. A validity verdict for an individual production measurement, based on the current condition of its path, lies beyond their scope.

The highlighted rows describe different things. The read-only software used in the recorded applications compares the paths of existing tests: it ranks candidate components by the number of affected paths that contain them, their presence in paths that stayed normal and the shift of their own reference measurements. Route switching, route confirmation, the sensitivity condition and ambiguity sets belong to the architecture of the provisional patent application and are planned for later versions of the software.

04 / Summary

RF-TraceGuard applies known methods to the measurement path

The parts have existed side by side for a long time. Instrument makers validate their instruments, analytics platforms watch streams of results, decision-risk methods are applied in advance, and diagnostic reasoning works on abstract tests and stored data. Each of them covers its own part of the problem. RF-TraceGuard takes the physical chain behind a measurement as the object of diagnosis between calibrations, and the measurement behind a pass or fail decision as the object of assessment.

RF-TraceGuard works alongside calibration, analytics and a station's own self-test, and each of them keeps its role.
