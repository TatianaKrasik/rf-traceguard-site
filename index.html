RF / ATE · Measurement integrity

RF-TraceGuard watches the test path between calibrations

A calibration certificate covers the instrument. Cables, connectors, switches and fixtures keep ageing after the calibration date, and a station with a drifting path keeps producing results that look perfectly normal.

RF-TraceGuard reads the reference history of the station together with its routine test data. Engineers see a persistent change in the measuring system early, learn which results need a second look and know where to start the inspection.

01 / Measurement integrity

The test setup deserves its own monitoring

Pass and fail limits describe the product. The condition of each cable, connection and signal path since the last calibration is a separate question, and the limits leave it open.

Take a shared cable whose insertion loss creeps up. Several product readings move in the same direction, and a retest through the same cable reproduces the same error. A separate reference check, together with the history of that path, tells a change in the station apart from a change in the product.

01

Describe the measurement path

Each test or reference result is linked to the known elements of its path: instruments, connections, cables, switches and fixtures. A common deviation across several results then has a clear context.

02

Keep a baseline

Qualified control measurements define the reference state. Later readings are compared with it under the same conditions, including measurement range and calibration context.

03

Follow the drift as well as the limits

Repeated reference readings are tracked for small, persistent shifts. The statistical tools are well established, and their value here comes from applying them to the condition of the measurement path.

04

Guide the inspection

Affected and unaffected observations are compared, and the components they share come under review. The result is a diagnostic pointer. An engineer checks it on the actual hardware and confirms the repair with a new measurement.

02 / Reading the evidence

Several results moving together point to a shared part of the setup

A change in a test result can come from the device, from the measurement equipment or from a connection between them. When several results move in the same direction, the engineer first wants to know which part of the setup they share.

RF-TraceGuard puts the recorded results, the reference checks and the path information side by side for that review. It points to the area worth inspecting, and the engineer confirms the cause on the equipment. A repeated measurement after the repair shows whether the reference response has recovered.

03 / Recorded measurement

Drift found in an inter-station study

In a recorded inter-station study, three comparable RF setups repeatedly measured the same stable control device. The early series fixed a reference profile, and every later result was compared with that profile and with the other two stations.

One station began to change steadily, mostly at the higher frequencies, while the other two stayed close to their initial values. Engineering checks traced the change to a worn cable. After the cable was replaced and the setup fully recalibrated, the series returned close to the reference profile.

Fig. 1. Maximum signed S21 magnitude deviation from a fixed reference profile, measured across twelve control series. The vertical line separates the pre-repair and post-repair series. Series 11 and 12 follow a cable replacement and full two-port recalibration. The plotted values come from the recorded measurements. Dashed horizontal lines mark the experimental warning (0.20 dB) and critical (0.30 dB) control levels of the study; the metrological tolerances of the setup remain in force.

Station personnel confirmed the cause

Monitoring caught the pattern and dated its onset. Engineering checks located the physical fault, and the post-repair measurements confirmed it.

04 / Development

Current software and further development

In use

RF-TraceGuard works with recorded test results and reference measurements. It compares them with an established baseline, follows changes from series to series and gives engineers the information they need to investigate a test setup.

It runs alongside the production test software. Station control and the final pass or fail decision stay with the existing system.

Further technical work

The wider technical design adds controlled reference paths and a way to narrow down a suspected fault when several components could explain the same readings.

These mechanisms belong to the next stage of development and are separate from the read-only software used in the recorded applications.
